WRITING AS TRANSLATION

Not essays written to perform intelligence.

Field Notes begin with something ordinary and stay with it long enough for a different angle to become visible.

An interaction.
A contradiction.
A behaviour that keeps repeating.
A sentence that will not leave you alone.
A question that changes shape the longer you stay with it.

Writing here is a way of paying closer attention.

Not to explain everything.

To make something more precise, more visible, or newly recognisable.

WHAT WRITING DOES HERE

Writing slows something down enough to see its structure.

What happened before the obvious thing happened.
What is being assumed.
What does not quite fit.
What is being said, and what sits underneath it.

Sometimes language changes what you are able to see.

That is the work.

COMMISSIONED WRITING

Bring something you cannot yet write.

You may already have the material.

Notes. Fragments. Voice recordings. A lived experience. A body of thought. A story you have never known how to tell. Something you need to say that keeps becoming smaller or less true every time you try to write it.

You do not need a finished brief.

We begin with what is actually there.

Through conversation and close inquiry, I look for the structure underneath the material — what carries weight, what repeats, what belongs, what is missing, and what the piece is really trying to say.

Then I write it.

YOU MIGHT BRING

  • an experience you want to put into words
  • a story you have never been able to tell properly
  • years of notes, fragments or voice recordings
  • an idea that is clear in your head but not on the page
  • something personal you need to articulate precisely
  • a body of thought that needs coherence
  • existing writing that does not yet sound true
  • something important that refuses a conventional format

You do not need to know what the final piece should look like.

You need to know there is something there.

WHAT IT MAY BECOME

Depending on the material:

  • a personal essay
  • a manifesto
  • a monologue
  • a personal constitution
  • a narrative piece
  • a letter
  • a spoken-word text
  • a founder or origin story
  • a long-form reflection
  • another written form that fits the material

The form is decided through the work.

The truth of the material comes first.

HOW A COMMISSION WORKS

01 — CONVERSATION

We begin with a focused conversation about what you are trying to express, what already exists, and why the current language is not enough.

02 — INQUIRY

I work through the material for structure, tension, meaning, voice and what sits underneath the obvious version.

03 — WRITING

I translate that into a coherent piece without flattening the complexity or making it sound like someone else.

04 — RETURN

You receive the finished written work, with a defined revision process agreed before we begin.

  • Ghostwritten “thought leadership” designed to manufacture authority.
  • Corporate copy dressed up as personal voice.
